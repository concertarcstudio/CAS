## Scene Overview

Scene 2 is the first VC-005 scene designed through the complete CAS Scene Design lifecycle before transition validation. It follows the identity-establishing work of Scene 1 by revealing greater emotional complexity and transforming listener curiosity into emotional investment.

## Engineering Objective

## Scene Thesis

## Evidence Plan

Transition validation should determine whether the locked design delivers the intended listener-state changes in practice. Evidence should address:

- distinction from Scene 1 at the opening;
- successful movement from curiosity into empathy;
- whether hope strengthens the emotional center;
- whether `Say Goodbye` protects rather than duplicates the emotional center;
- whether the Recovery entry restores energy without emotional whiplash;
- whether `Hurricane` completes Recovery and earns the closing descent;
- whether the closer creates anticipation rather than premature resolution.

## Initial Risks

- `Medusa (Stone)` → `The One` may create a large tonal shift despite its intended humanization function.
- The relationship sequence could become emotionally concentrated if individual transitions do not create enough functional distinction.
- `Say Goodbye` may extend the acceptance register too long if its reflective role is not clearly differentiated.
- The transition into Recovery may undercut the emotional center if it is too abrupt.
- `Hurricane` may create excessive scale immediately before the introspective closer.
- Final scene-boundary validation cannot be completed until the opening of Scene 3 is designed.

## Certification Criteria

Scene 2 will be eligible for certification only when:

- TA-0016 through TA-0022 receive final Engineering Decision Status;
- all intended movement-level listener-state changes are supported by evidence;
- credible Red Team alternatives fail to produce a stronger scene;
- deferred scene-boundary validation is resolved at the appropriate review level;
- repository validation and indexes pass CAS Automation;
- the full scene survives systems and listener review.
